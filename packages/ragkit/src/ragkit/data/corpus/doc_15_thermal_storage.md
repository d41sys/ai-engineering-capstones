# Industrial Thermal Energy Storage (TES) and High-Temperature Molten Salt

Decarbonizing industrial process heat requires decoupling electrical generation from steam and thermal delivery. Thermal Energy Storage (TES) systems store energy in the form of sensible heat, latent heat (phase-change materials), or thermochemical reactions, providing flexible power-to-heat solutions that absorb intermittent renewable energy.

## Sensible, Latent, and Molten Salt Technologies

TES technologies are categorized by their physical storage mechanisms:
1. Sensible Heat Storage: Utilizes liquid or solid mediums that change temperature without phase transition. Molten nitrate salts (e.g., 60% NaNO3 / 40% KNO3) operating between 290°C and 565°C are widely deployed in concentrated solar power (CSP) and retrofitted thermal plants. Solid media such as crushed volcanic rock, concrete blocks, and cast ceramic bricks can store heat above 800°C for industrial kilns.
2. Latent Heat Storage: Utilizes phase change materials (PCMs) absorbing high amounts of latent heat at near-constant temperatures during solid-to-liquid transitions (e.g., paraffin waxes or molten salts with eutectic compositions).
3. Steam Accumulators: Pressurized water vessels providing rapid steam delivery over short discharge windows to buffer industrial batch manufacturing loads.

## Grid Flexibility and Sector Coupling

High-temperature thermal storage systems equipped with resistive heating elements can absorb surplus renewable electricity at high charging capacities. By storing electrical energy as high-grade heat and discharging it through steam generators or Organic Rankine Cycles (ORC), TES provides bidirectional sector coupling: acting as a controllable load during negative pricing hours, and displacing natural gas boiler combustion during peak electricity periods.
## Solid Media Storage and High-Temperature Heat Exchangers

In addition to molten salts, solid media thermal energy storage systems provide low-cost heat storage at temperatures exceeding 800°C for industrial manufacturing. Crushed refractory basalt rock or cast ceramic blocks are housed in heavily insulated steel silos. Air or inert nitrogen gas circulates through the packed bed, transferring heat from high-capacity electrical heating resistors during periods of surplus grid electricity. During thermal discharge, reverse air circulation extracts the stored thermal energy, feeding industrial steam boilers, ceramic drying kilns, or calcination processes, achieving overall round-trip thermal efficiencies exceeding 90% while fully decoupling industrial heat consumption from fossil fuels.
